# Symbolic Computation–Guided Proofs of Conjectured Identities

With the aid of our symbolic computation framework, we have completed **mathematical proofs and Lean formalizations of eight main identities across seven groups of problems**, covering harmonic-number series, hypergeometric summation, special-value evaluations, and q-series identities.

This repository presents the original conjectures, the main mathematical ideas behind their proofs, and the role of our framework. We use parameterized families to reveal the structure of the identities, then search for summation certificates, verify algebraic relations, and perform exact coefficient elimination. Convergence arguments, boundary evaluations, and analytic uniqueness complete the proofs. Every final Lean proof uses proof fragments generated by the shared framework.

Throughout, we write

$$
H_n^{(r)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac1{j^r},\qquad H_n=H_n^{(1)},\qquad H_0^{(r)}=0.
$$

Here $\zeta$ denotes the Riemann zeta function and $\Gamma$ the Gamma function.

## 1. Sun's harmonic-number Conjecture 8

The conjecture appears as Conjecture 8, equation (27), in Zhi-Wei Sun's *Series with summands involving harmonic numbers*:

$$
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{4k}{2k}\left(\frac{x(1-x)}4\right)^k
(2H_{4k}-3H_{2k}+H_k)
=\frac{\sqrt{1-x}}{2x-1}\log(1-x),
\qquad \frac{1-\sqrt2}{2}\le x<\frac12.
$$

**Proof idea.** We introduce a parameterized hypergeometric generating function and derive its Gauss differential equation from the coefficient recurrence. A candidate closed form satisfies the same equation; uniqueness of analytic solutions identifies the generating function. Differentiation with respect to the parameter produces the required harmonic-number combination. The left endpoint is handled through the limit of the ordered partial sums.

**Role of the framework.** It generates and verifies a polynomial certificate for the differential equation after clearing denominators.

## 2. Sun's Conjecture 3.1: two harmonic-number series identities

Let

$$
A_k=\frac{\binom{2k}{k}^{2}\binom{3k}{k}}{(-192)^k}.
$$

The two identities are

$$
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}A_k\left[(5k+1)(H_{2k}-H_k)+\frac{3k+1}{2k+1}\right]
=\frac{4\log3}{\sqrt3\,\pi},
$$

and

$$
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}A_k\left[(5k+1)(H_{3k}-H_k)+\frac{4k+3}{6k+3}\right]
=\frac{4}{3\sqrt3\,\pi}\log\frac{64}{3}.
$$

**Proof idea.** The first identity combines differentiation of Guillera's parameter identity with a specific evaluation of Zhou's companion identity. The argument involves hypergeometric functions, cubic modular parameters, and their values at special points. The second identity follows from an exact linear combination of the first identity and the companion identity.

**Role of the framework.** It searches for the rational Wilf–Zeilberger (WZ) certificate used in the first proof and performs exact coefficient matching and linear elimination for the second.

## 3. Sun's Conjecture 3.5: an identity involving second-order harmonic numbers

Let

$$
B_n=\frac{\binom{2n}{n}^{2}\binom{4n}{2n}}{(-12288)^n},
\qquad D_n=36H_{4n}^{(2)}-9H_{2n}^{(2)}-H_n^{(2)}.
$$

The identity is

$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_n\left[(28n+3)D_n+\frac{12}{4n+1}\right]
=\frac{16\pi}{3\sqrt3}.
$$

**Proof idea.** We embed the second-order harmonic-number expression in a symmetric parameter family. WZ summation and Dougall's formula establish a parameter identity, from which we extract the second-order coefficient.

**Role of the framework.** Starting from the shift ratios of a Gamma parameter family, it searches for a rational WZ certificate and generates an algebraic proof after clearing denominators. The full argument also establishes the boundary limits, convergence in a parameter neighborhood, and the validity of coefficient extraction.

## 4. Sun's Conjecture 3.8: a series for ζ(3)

The identity is

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^3\binom{2n}{n}^{3}}
\left[(21n-8)(2H_{3n-1}-H_{n-1})-\frac{4(27n-11)}{3(3n-1)}\right]
=3\zeta(3).
$$

**Proof idea.** We construct a parameterized generating function whose derivative at zero gives the target series. A parameter-shift relation determines finite sums at negative integer parameters. Uniqueness for bounded holomorphic functions then establishes the generating identity, and termwise differentiation yields the result.

**Role of the framework.** It solves for a polynomial certificate in the parameter-shift equation, connecting the generating function to an exact telescoping relation.

## 5. Sun's Conjecture 3.11: a weight-five special-value identity

Let $P(n)=21n^3-22n^2+8n-1$. The identity is

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{256^n}{n^7\binom{2n}{n}^{7}}
\left[14P(n)(H_{2n-1}-H_{n-1})-63n^2+44n-8\right]
=\frac{93}{2}\zeta(5)+\pi^4\log2.
$$

**Proof idea.** Au's parameter identities express the target harmonic-number series in terms of expansion coefficients near zero. Resolving removable singularities, differentiating with respect to the parameter, and evaluating auxiliary series give the stated combination of special values.

**Role of the framework.** It searches for and verifies the WZ certificate associated with Au's Example XV, generating algebraic lemmas used in the final formal proof.

## 6. Sun's Conjecture 4.2, first identity: a sum of Gamma-function derivatives

For $x>0$, define

$$
f_2(x)=\frac{\left(\zeta(2)-\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(x+j)^{-2}\right)\Gamma(x)^2}{2x\Gamma(2x)}.
$$

The identity is

$$
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}f_2'(k)=\frac{29}{27}\zeta(5).
$$

**Proof idea.** Building on Patel's parameter construction, we start from a four-parameter WZ identity. A linear combination of third-order parameter coefficients extracts the target derivative series, which is then simplified using relations among multiple zeta values of weight five.

**Role of the framework.** It searches for WZ certificates, performs exact matching and elimination of third-order parameter coefficients, and computes rational linear combinations of the required zeta relations.

## 7. The first modulo 9 Kanade–Russell identity

The identity is

$$
\sum_{r,s\ge0}\frac{q^{r^2+3rs+3s^2}}{(q;q)_r(q^3;q^3)_s}
=\frac1{(q,q^3,q^6,q^8;q^9)_\infty}.
$$

Here $(a;q)_n=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(1-aq^j)$; the infinite subscript denotes the corresponding infinite product, and notation with several parameters denotes the product of the corresponding factors. The completed Lean proof establishes this equality as an identity of formal power series.

**Proof idea.** Our proof variant follows Xia's construction using divided differences of products and a common recurrence. Reconstructed q-hypergeometric certificates establish finite summation relations; boundary conditions and uniqueness then connect the double sum to the product.

**Role of the framework.** It searches for and verifies two rational q-hypergeometric certificates and generates the corresponding polynomial proofs.

## From symbolic computation to formal proof

The framework provides ordinary and q-hypergeometric certificate search, verification of differential-equation residuals, exact coefficient elimination, and Lean lemma generation. Mathematical analysis determines the parameter families and proof strategies. The full proofs also establish convergence, boundary behavior, interchange of differentiation and summation, and uniqueness.

The unified verification record dated September 22, 2026 reports successful builds of all eight final theorems and successful checks that their proofs depend on the generated lemmas. Their axiom dependencies are limited to Lean's standard `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`, with no dependency on `sorryAx` or additional axioms.
